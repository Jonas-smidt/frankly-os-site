That folder is your seat: your profile lives there, and your learnings land there.

When it's connected, open the team folders and check that Claude reaches them.

And a crew of specialists behind them.

A specialist is a colleague with one job and one standard: an editor who cuts, a researcher who brings sources, a positioning strategist, a judge whose only task is to say whether the work is good enough. You never pick one from a menu — you describe the work in plain English and Claude hands it to the right seat, the way you would hand a draft to whoever on the team is best at that particular thing.

What changes for you in practice: you can now ask “is this good enough to send?” and get a real second opinion — someone reading your draft against Frankly’s standard instead of agreeing with you. That did not exist before.

The specialists run in Cowork and in Claude Code — never in the Chat tab. If you are sitting in Chat you will see the skills and no specialists, and nothing is broken: open the same work in Cowork and the crew is there.

Installing the plugin sets it up for you, on your own machine — it does not set it up for anyone else, so each colleague runs steps 4 and 5 themselves. When Jonas publishes a new version, he will say so.

How the system learns from you

Everything you make is stamped with your name. Not for control — the system needs to know who discovered what when it learns. Your annoyance today is a rule tomorrow.

Say it, Claude logs it

Correct Claude, prefer one version over another, or get annoyed by the same thing twice — and it logs that as a learning in your own team folder. It tells you in one line that it did. You file nothing. If you'd rather it didn't, say so, and it won't.

That's how what you learn reaches the system — so the next person doesn't hit the same wall.

Nothing stops you

If you write about coverage, pricing or legal terms, Claude marks it and keeps working:

⚑ claims-review ved udgangen

(Danish for "claims review at the exit" — the system's canonical marker.) The flag means Jonas reviews it before it gets published. The gate sits at the exit — never in your process.

The specialists advise — they do not approve. Part of the crew stays on Jonas's machine on purpose: the connector guard and the QA runner need his gate scripts, and without them they would tell you a gate or a check had happened when nothing had been checked. So a specialist saying your draft is good is a good second opinion, not a company approval.

Publishing goes through Jonas. Anything that leaves the house — franklyinsure.com, customer and partner sends, deploys — he picks up. The cockpit is his machine; you don't need it.

FAQ

Do I need to code?

No. You write in plain English, the way you'd write to a colleague.

What if I make a mistake?

You can't break anything. You can't publish and you can't delete in Drive. Worst case is a bad draft.

Why does it ask who I am?

Because your work is stamped with your name, and because a learning has to be traceable to a person.

Can I write about coverage and pricing?

Yes. Write freely. It gets flagged for review — not stopped.

I only see the skills — where are the specialists?

You are in the Chat tab. The specialists run in Cowork and in Claude Code, and Chat greys them out. Same work, same conversation — just open it in Cowork.

Do I have to choose which specialist to use?

No. Describe the work and Claude picks the seat. If you want a specific one, ask for it by what it does — “let the editor cut this”.

Does a specialist approve my work?

No. It gives you an honest second opinion. Publishing, sending and deploying still stop with Jonas.

Who do I ask when something's off?

Jonas. Or ask Claude to log it with log-feedback — it reaches him either way.
